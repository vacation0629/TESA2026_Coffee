## 1. Executive Summary & Context
In Chumphon Province, the expansion of local coffee cafes, roasteries, and tourist processing facilities generates large daily volumes of spent coffee grounds (SCG). Rather than discarding this byproduct into landfills, thermochemical conversion of SCG into porous activated carbon offers an attractive circular-economy pathway.

Simultaneously, industrial dye pollution—specifically **Rhodamine B (RhB)**, a toxic, carcinogenic synthetic xanthene dye used in textiles, paper, and leather—poses severe ecological hazards when discharged into water bodies. Conventional commercial activated carbons (derived from coal or coconut shells) require expensive physical activation furnaces operating under continuous inert gas ($N_2$ or $Ar$) purge and high energy costs.

This study by KMITL Prince of Chumphon Campus establishes an affordable, energy-efficient chemical activation protocol to produce high-performance activated carbon from local Pathio (Chumphon) spent coffee grounds.

---

## 2. Experimental Synthesis & Innovative Activation Protocol
- **Feedstock Source:** Spent coffee grounds (SCG) collected fresh from a local coffee shop in Pathio District, Chumphon Province, Thailand.
- **Pre-Treatment (Acid Washing):**
  - Washed with **1.0 M $H_2SO_4$ (Sulfuric Acid)** prior to thermal processing.
  - *Critical Chemical Innovation:* The acid leaching removes alkali ash metals, opens cellular pores, and enables clean pyrolysis **under a limited-oxygen atmosphere without requiring continuous inert gas ($N_2$) flow**.
- **Carbonization Step:**
  - Heated at **600 °C for 2.0 hours** in a closed limited-oxygen chamber. The resulting biochar was completely ash-free.
- **Activation Step (Microwave-Assisted KOH Activation):**
  - Biochar impregnated with **Potassium Hydroxide ($KOH$)** chemical activating agent.
  - Activated using rapid **microwave heating**, which drastically reduces energy consumption and synthesis duration compared to traditional 4–8 hour electrical tube furnace heating.

## 3. Batch Adsorption Performance & Optimization Parameters
The prepared coffee-derived activated carbon was tested for the aqueous removal of Rhodamine B dye across multiple operational parameters:

| Operational Parameter | Tested Range | Optimal Operational Point | Physical / Chemical Behavior |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Initial Dye Concentration ($C_0$)** | 3.0 – 11.0 mg/L | — | Adsorption capacity scaled with concentration gradient driving force |
| **Solution pH** | pH 3.0 – 11.0 | **pH 7.0 (Neutral)** | At neutral pH, surface charges favor electrostatic interaction and H-bonding |
| **Contact Time** | 15 – 150 minutes | **Equilibrium reached within 60–90 min** | Rapid initial uptake in micropores and mesopores |
| **Maximum Adsorption Capacity ($q_{\max}$)** | — | **16.0 mg/g of adsorbent** | Complete monolayer coverage of accessible active sites |

## 4. Adsorption Isotherm & Reaction Kinetics
- **Isotherm Modeling:** The equilibrium data demonstrated an excellent fit to the **Langmuir Isotherm model** ($R^2 > 0.98$), indicating homogeneous monolayer adsorption onto defined active surface sites without lateral interaction between adsorbed dye molecules.
- **Kinetic Modeling:** The dynamic adsorption process obeyed the **Pseudo-Second-Order kinetic model**, proving that **chemisorption** (covalent-like valence forces, $\pi-\pi$ electron donor-acceptor interactions between the aromatic rings of Rhodamine B and the graphene layers of the carbonized coffee matrix) is the rate-limiting mechanism.

## 5. Circular Economy & Quality Parameters in Real Life
1. **Zero-Waste Value Addition in Coffee Production:**
   - Demonstrates that post-consumer coffee waste in Chumphon can be transformed into an effective water purification media.
2. **Simplified, Low-Cost Technology for Local Communities:**
   - Eliminates high-cost industrial infrastructure ($N_2$ cylinders, high-temperature rotary kilns) through 1M $H_2SO_4$ acid washing and microwave-assisted activation.
3. **Application in Coffee Mill Wastewater Remediation:**
   - Coffee processing produces highly colored, high-COD wastewater (from mucilage fermentation and washings). This low-cost carbon can be deployed directly on-farm in wastewater polishing ponds to adsorb polyphenols, tannins, and organic colorants before environmental discharge.
