## 1. Executive Summary & Problem Context
In primary coffee wet and dry processing mills across Chumphon, massive quantities of agricultural solid residues are generated annually:
- **Coffee Cherry Pulp (CCP):** From wet depulping.
- **Coffee Parchment (CPM / Husk):** From dry hulling and milling.
- **Expired / Defective Green Coffee Beans (ECB):** Sub-standard, insect-damaged, or over-stored green beans unfit for commercial roasting.

Improper disposal of these residues leads to severe environmental degradation, foul odor, leachate runoff into waterways, and methane emissions. Simultaneously, commercial coffee roasting and drying operations rely heavily on fossil fuels—primarily imported **Liquid Petroleum Gas (LPG)** and electricity—which incur substantial economic costs and carbon footprints (as quantified in Research 11).

This KMITL Chumphon study explored the conversion of these coffee residues into standardized **Coffee Biomass Pellets (CBPs)** via single pelletization and co-pelletization blends, and evaluated their downdraft gasification into **Synthesis Gas (Syngas)** for clean thermal heat generation in coffee drying and roasting facilities.

---

## 2. Experimental Methodology & Pellet Formulation
- **Feedstock Types:**
  1. Coffee Cherry Pulp (CCP)
  2. Coffee Parchment (CPM)
  3. Expired Green Coffee Beans (ECB)
- **Pelletization Formulations:**
  - 100% Single feedstocks: 100% CCP, 100% CPM.
  - Co-pelletization blends: CCP:CPM ratios (e.g., 80:20, 60:40, 50:50, 40:60, 20:80) and additions of ECB.
- **Pellet Quality Testing:**
  - Moisture content, unit density, bulk density, mechanical durability index (tumbler test).
  - Ultimate and proximate analysis (volatile matter, fixed carbon, ash content).
  - Higher Heating Value (HHV) and Lower Heating Value (LHV) via bomb calorimetry.
  - Ash fusion temperature, slagging and fouling indices ($R_s$, $B/A$ ratio).
- **Thermal Gasification System:**
  - Industrial-scale throat-type downdraft gasifier with air blast.
  - Syngas cleanup (cyclone separator, condenser, biomass filter).
  - Online gas chromatography (GC-TCD) for Syngas composition ($CO$, $H_2$, $CH_4$, $CO_2$, $N_2$) and flame temperature / thermal heat output.

## 3. Key Empirical Findings & Biomass Fuel Metrics
1. **Pellet Physicochemical Characteristics:**
   - **Pellet Bulk Density:** Densification increased bulk density from $< 200 \text{ kg/m}^3$ (loose husks) to $> 600 - 680 \text{ kg/m}^3$, dramatically lowering transportation and storage footprint.
   - **Mechanical Durability:** Co-pelletized CCP:CPM blends exhibited durability indices $> 95\%$, meeting international ISO 17225-6 standards for non-woody biomass pellets.
   - **Energy Density / Heating Values:**
     - High calorific value: **17.5 – 19.8 MJ/kg (HHV)**.
     - Blends containing expired coffee beans (ECB) exhibited even higher calorific values ($> 20\text{ MJ/kg}$) due to the residual lipids/coffee oils inside bean endosperms.
2. **Gasification Performance & Syngas Quality:**
   - Downdraft gasification produced clean, stable syngas with high concentrations of combustible gases:
     - **Carbon Monoxide ($CO$):** 16 – 21% vol
     - **Hydrogen ($H_2$):** 10 – 15% vol
     - **Methane ($CH_4$):** 1.5 – 3.2% vol
     - Balance: $CO_2$ (10–13%) and $N_2$ (48–55%).
   - **Syngas Heating Value:** Lower heating value (LHV) of **4.5 – 5.6 $\text{MJ/Nm}^3$**.
   - **Thermal Heat Generation:** Sustained stable combustion flame temperatures of **850 – 1,050 °C**, providing sufficient thermal energy for industrial air heat exchangers.
3. **Slagging & Ash Melting Management:**
   - Pure CCP ash contains potassium and alkali salts ($K_2O$), which lower ash melting point and cause clinker/slagging on grate bars if gasified pure.
   - **Co-pelletization Solution:** Blending CPM (parchment, rich in silica and fibrous lignin) with CCP significantly increased ash fusion temperature, completely suppressing slagging and clinker agglomeration in the downdraft reactor.

## 4. Real-Life Closed-Loop Application in Coffee Processing
- **Replacing LPG in Roasting and Drying:**
  - Paper 11 showed that coffee roasting consumes substantial LPG (accounting for ~0.41 kg CO₂e / kg coffee).
  - Syngas produced from on-farm coffee pulp and husks can be piped directly into coffee roaster burners and mechanical convective drying beds.
- **Economic & Environmental Closed Loop:**
  - 1 ton of processed fresh cherries yields ~400 kg of wet pulp and 120 kg of parchment.
  - Converting this waste into pellets satisfies **100% of the thermal energy required for bean drying and roasting**, eliminating LPG fuel bills and converting a waste liability into on-site renewable heat.
