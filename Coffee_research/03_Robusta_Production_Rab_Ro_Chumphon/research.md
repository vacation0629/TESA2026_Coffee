## 1. Executive Summary & Context
Chumphon Province is the largest Robusta coffee production hub in Thailand, encompassing over 129,829 rai (representing roughly 60% of Thailand's southern coffee area). Within Chumphon, Rab Ro (Rubror) Subdistrict in Tha Sae District represents a core coffee cultivation epicenter. However, smallholder farmers often face high production costs (fertilizers, pesticides, hired harvesting labor) and variable bean quality, leading to lower net farm incomes and vulnerability to market price fluctuations.

This empirical field study surveyed **312 coffee farming households** in Rab Ro Subdistrict using structured questionnaires, in-depth interviews, descriptive statistics, and Chi-Square ($\chi^2$) hypothesis testing at statistical significance levels $p < 0.05$ and $p < 0.01$.

---

## 2. Farm Demographic & Economic Baseline (Chumphon Real-Life Baseline)
- **Gender & Demographics:** 52.6% male, 47.4% female; average age **47.38 years**; 69.2% attained primary education or lower.
- **Household & Labor:** Average household size **3.66 persons**; average labor dedicated to coffee farming **4.55 persons** (heavily reliant on hired seasonal labor during peak harvest).
- **Farming Experience:** Average **20.74 years** of Robusta farming experience.
- **Group Membership:** Overwhelming majority were **not members of any formal coffee cooperative or community enterprise**, operating as independent smallholders.
- **Land Holding:** Average Robusta plantation area of **14.41 rai** per household.
- **Financial Metrics (2018 Economic Baseline):**
  - **Gross Coffee Income:** 15,276.66 THB / rai / year
  - **Production Expenses (Input costs):** 4,721.61 THB / rai / year
  - **Net Coffee Margin:** ~10,555.05 THB / rai / year
  - **Capital Financing:** 100% self-financed (relying on personal savings rather than institutional agricultural loans).
  - **Marketing Channel:** Sold almost entirely to **local middlemen (พ่อค้าคนกลาง)** as dry unhulled cherries or bulk green beans.

### Technology Knowledge vs. Practice Gap:
- **Knowledge Level:** 85.9% of farmers exhibited a **High Knowledge Level** regarding recommended coffee production technologies (mean knowledge score 16.96 out of 19.00).
- **Action Gap:** Despite knowing optimal agricultural practices, actual field execution remained only "partially compliant."

## 4. Crucial Finding: The Harvest Quality Bottleneck in Real Life
The study uncovered a major socio-economic barrier directly impairing coffee quality in real-life farming:
1. **Failure of Selective Picking:**
   - The agricultural standard explicitly recommends **selective picking (เก็บเฉพาะผลสุกสีแดงเต็มที่)** at peak ripeness to optimize sugar content, reduce astringency/bitterness, and avoid green astringent off-flavors.
   - In practice, farmers hire outside casual labor who are compensated **by weight (Baht per kilogram of cherries harvested)**.
   - Consequently, laborers perform **strip-picking (การรูดกิ่ง)**, harvesting green (unripe), semi-ripe (yellow), fully ripe (red/purple), and overripe (black/fermented) cherries simultaneously to maximize weight harvested per hour.
2. **Statistical Anomaly in Hypothesis Testing:**
   - While education, household size, farming experience, income, labor, and knowledge were statistically correlated ($p < 0.05$ and $p < 0.01$) with planting and maintenance practices, **they showed NO statistical correlation with harvesting practice compliance**.
   - **Reason:** Even highly educated and experienced farmers could not enforce selective harvesting on hired laborers because paying higher piece rates for selective picking was not economically rewarded by local middlemen, who buy bulk coffee on flat weight without price premiums for 100% ripe red cherries.

---

## 5. Strategic Quality Improvement Recommendations
- **Transition from Flat Commodity Pricing to Quality Incentive Tiering:** Introduce farmgate moisture, defect, and ripeness grading where farmers receive a 15–30% price premium for lots containing $>90\%$ red cherries.
- **Group Aggregation:** Shift from individual middleman dependency to community enterprises (such as Ban Tham Sing model) to invest in collective washing, density grading, and parabolic solar drying.
