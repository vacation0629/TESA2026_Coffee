## 1. Executive Summary & Research Background
*Coffea canephora* (Robusta) is an obligate cross-pollinated (allogamous) tree crop with high genetic heterozygosity. For decades, the Department of Agriculture (DOA) has selected and bred Robusta clones adapted to the Southern microclimate at the Chumphon Horticultural Research Center (CHRC). 

Historically, Thai Robusta cultivars were classified into two main genetic groups:
1. **Indigenous / Traditional Thai Clones (กลุ่มพันธุ์ไทยพื้นเมือง):** Typified by **Chumphon 1 (ชุมพร 1)** and **L69**. Characterized by large bean sizes, high out-turn ratios, and later harvest cycles, but slower initial canopy establishment.
2. **Introduced / Exotic Germplasm (กลุ่มพันธุ์ต่างประเทศ):** Typified by **Chumphon 2 (ชุมพร 2)**, **Chumphon 3**, **Chumphon 4**, and **Chumphon 5**. Characterized by vigorous early vegetative growth, fast initial fruiting (precocity), and high cherry count, but smaller bean screen sizes and early harvest cycles overlapping with peak Southern monsoons.

This long-term breeding program (evaluating collections from across 80 indigenous lines and exotic introductions) establishes the agronomic and bean quality benchmarks for Chumphon 1, Chumphon 2, Chumphon 5, and next-generation recurrent selection hybrids.

## 3. Deep Analysis of Real-Life Quality Parameters

### A. The "Harvest Window vs. Rainy Season" Quality Factor:
- The research report highlights a critical practical interaction between variety genetics and weather risk:
  - **Chumphon 2's Vulnerability:** Because Chumphon 2 ripens early (October–December), its peak harvest coincides directly with the heavy Southern monsoon season in Chumphon. Farmers harvesting Chumphon 2 are forced to sun-dry fresh cherries in torrential rain and persistent cloud cover, drastically elevating the risk of bean fermentation rotting, black bean defects, and mold contamination (*Aspergillus*).
  - **Chumphon 1's Advantage:** Chumphon 1 ripens later (November to January/February). Harvesting occurs when the monsoon subsides, allowing clean sun-drying under bright clear skies with much lower atmospheric humidity, naturally protecting green bean sensory quality.

### B. Out-Turn Ratio (% Out-turn) Economics:
- **Definition:** The percentage of processed dry green coffee beans (at 12% moisture) recovered from a given weight of fresh coffee cherries.
  $$\% \text{Out-turn} = \frac{\text{Weight of Dry Green Beans}}{\text{Weight of Fresh Ripe Cherries}} \times 100$$
- **Real-Life Value:**
  - Standard baseline: ~20% (meaning 5 kg of fresh cherries yields 1 kg of green bean).
  - High-performing clones (**Chumphon 1, Chumphon 5, and L69 hybrids** exceed **21.0–21.5%**).
  - This 1.5% out-turn difference translates to **75–100 kg of additional saleable green coffee per hectare** without extra fertilizer or land costs.

### C. Standardized Sieve Sizing (ISO 4150-1991 / Thai Agricultural Standard TAS 5700):
The report strictly grades green coffee using standard test sieves:
- **Size 1 (Large / Extra Bold):** $\ge 7.1\text{ mm}$ (Sieve No. 18)
- **Size 2 (Bold / Medium):** $6.3 \text{ mm} \le \text{size} < 7.1\text{ mm}$ (Sieve No. 16)
- **Size 3 (Small):** $5.6 \text{ mm} \le \text{size} < 6.3\text{ mm}$ (Sieve No. 14)
- **Size 4 (Under-grade / Screenings):** $< 5.6\text{ mm}$
- Clones with $>60\%$ retention on Sieve No. 16–18 represent premium commercial grades that roast evenly without scorching smaller beans.

## 4. Polyclonal Planting Requirements for Real Farms
Because Robusta coffee possesses **gametophytic self-incompatibility (GSI)**, flowers cannot self-fertilize. 
- **DOA Golden Rule:** A farmer must **NEVER plant a monoculture clone** of a single variety.
- **Field Recommendation:** Farmers must plant a **polyclonal orchard containing at least 3 to 4 compatible recommended clones (e.g., Chumphon 1 + Chumphon 2 + Chumphon 5 + Chumphon 84-4)** interspersed in alternating rows. This ensures synchronized flowering, maximum cross-pollination by wind and bees, high fruit set ($>75\%$), and eliminates sterile hollow peaberries.
