## 1. Executive Summary & Context
Spent coffee grounds (SCG) represent the massive solid waste residue generated during commercial beverage preparation and instant coffee extraction. Over 6 million tons of SCG are generated globally each year, usually ending up in landfills where anaerobic decomposition produces greenhouse gases (methane). However, SCG is rich in residual bioactive compounds, polyphenols, caffeine, melanoidins, and dietary fibers.

This research by KMITL investigates how two critical operational variables:
1. **Initial Coffee Roasting Levels:** Light, Medium, and Dark roasts.
2. **Sequential Hydrothermal Brewing Cycles:** Successive hot-water extraction passes (Cycle 1 vs. subsequent cycles).

affect the retention, stability, and extractability of bioactive compounds (Total Phenolic Content - TPC, Total Flavonoid Content - TFC, Chlorogenic Acids - CGA, and Caffeine) within spent coffee grounds.

---

## 2. Experimental Methodology
- **Raw Materials:** Standardized Arabica and Robusta lots processed under controlled industrial roasting profiles:
  - **Light Roast:** Roasted to lower bean temperature prior to first crack completion; preserves native phenolic hydroxycinnamic acids.
  - **Medium Roast:** Standard commercial roast balance.
  - **Dark Roast:** Higher temperature past second crack; extensive pyrolysis and Maillard reaction yielding high melanoidin polymers.
- **Brewing Extraction Protocol:** Standard hot-water brewing under pressurized and gravitational cycles, followed by sequential re-extraction brewing passes to monitor exhaustion curves of bioactive compounds.
- **Analytical Metrics:**
  - Total Phenolic Content (TPC, Folin-Ciocalteu assay, mg GAE/g)
  - Total Flavonoid Content (TFC, aluminum chloride colorimetry, mg QE/g)
  - Chlorogenic Acid isomers (3-CQA, 4-CQA, 5-CQA via HPLC)
  - Residual Caffeine concentration (HPLC-DAD)
  - Antioxidant Capacities (DPPH, ABTS radical scavenging, and FRAP ferric reducing power)
  - Browning Index / Melanoidin optical density ($A_{420\text{ nm}}$)

## 3. Key Findings & Empirical Data
1. **The First Brewing Cycle Dynamics (Soluble Fraction Depletion):**
   - The **first brewing cycle extracts the vast majority (>70–85%) of easily accessible, water-soluble polar bioactive compounds** into the beverage cup, including free caffeine and monomeric chlorogenic acids.
   - Subsequent extraction cycles (Cycle 2, Cycle 3) demonstrate sharply diminishing extraction yields in the liquid phase.
2. **Bioactive Compound Persistence in Spent Grounds:**
   - Despite heavy extraction during primary brewing, the solid residue (SCG matrix) retains substantial quantities of bound polyphenols, insoluble antioxidants, and trapped hydrophobic components embedded within the lignocellulosic cell wall matrix.
3. **Diminishing Influence of Initial Roasting Level Post-Brewing:**
   - Prior to brewing, roasting level heavily dictates the chemical profile of the bean: light roasts possess significantly higher free chlorogenic acids, whereas dark roasts exhibit extensive thermal degradation of CGAs into chlorogenic acid lactones and complex high-molecular-weight melanoidins.
   - **Crucial Research Insight:** Once brewing occurs, the **differences in bioactive compound concentrations between light-, medium-, and dark-roast SCGs become significantly attenuated (diminished)**. The extraction process removes most soluble free phenolics regardless of roast level, leaving behind a core baseline of recalcitrant, cell-wall-bound functional metabolites in the SCG.
4. **Browning and Polymer Index:**
   - Dark-roast SCGs retain significantly higher browning indices ($A_{420}$) and Maillard polymers, which contribute substantial non-phenolic antioxidant capacity (radical scavenging through melanoidin structures).

## 4. Real-Life Coffee Quality & Processing Parameters
| Parameter | Light Roast | Medium Roast | Dark Roast | Operational / Quality Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Native CGA Retention** | Highest | Moderate | Lowest (extensive pyrolysis) | Acidity, astringency, cup brightness |
| **Melanoidin Formation** | Low | Moderate | Highest ($A_{420}$ peak) | Body, bitterness, brown color, crema stability |
| **First-Cycle Extraction Rate** | High solubility | High solubility | High solubility | Strips 75–85% soluble caffeine and phenolics |
| **Residual SCG Bound Phenolics** | Persistent in fibers | Persistent in fibers | Persistent in fibers | Valuable secondary feedstock for upcycling |

---

## 5. Industrial Upcycling & Waste Valorization Implication
- Demonstrates that spent grounds from commercial coffee shops (regardless of whether the shop serves light, medium, or dark roasts) serve as a consistent, uniform feedstock for:
  - Antioxidant nutraceutical extracts and cosmetic anti-aging formulations.
  - Functional food fortification (antioxidant dietary fiber).
  - Precursor bio-adsorbents for wastewater treatment (as corroborated in Research 07).
